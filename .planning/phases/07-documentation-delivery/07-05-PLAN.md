<objective>
Write the section a skeptic would have written, and prove each of its six required parts is there.

Purpose: ROADMAP Phase 7 criterion 3, which no other plan in this phase carries. The criterion names
six things and names them for a reason -- it is the one criterion written from the position of a
reader trying to take the result apart. A limitations section that lists three of the six and
paraphrases the rest is the failure mode, because the ones that get dropped are always the ones that
cost the most to admit.

This is a portfolio repository whose entire claim is that it did causal inference honestly. The
first screen already refuses to hide that the revenue interval covers zero. This section is the other
half of that posture: the project states what would break its own claim, in its own words, before a
reviewer has to find it.

The depth already exists -- `reports/policy.md` §13 is eight bullets long and is tested. D-04 says the
README links rather than restates. But criterion 3 asks for the section to be **present in the
README**, and a reviewer who reads only the front door is exactly the reader this criterion protects.
The resolution: the README carries the criterion's six, each short, each checkable, and links §13 as
the longer list. That is not a restatement of the depth document; it is the subset the criterion
names.

Output: the README's limitations section, and two tests behind it.
</objective>

Open the section with one sentence that sets the posture: a reader who wants to disbelieve this
result should start here, and nothing above repairs any of it. That sentence is doing work -- it tells
a reviewer that the list is the project's own rather than a compliance exercise.

**Then the six items, one bullet each, in the criterion's own order.** Each bullet leads with a bold
phrase naming the limitation, then one or two sentences of plain English. No bullet may end with a
clause that recovers the claim.

1. **The data is from 2008.** Email norms, spam filtering, inbox placement, list hygiene and what
   customers expect from a retailer have all moved since. Nothing here establishes that the same
   customers would respond the same way to the same email today, or that the segmentation the model
   found still exists.

2. **A single two-week observation window.** Visits, orders and revenue are counted in the fortnight
   after the send and nowhere else. A policy that pulls a purchase forward by three weeks and a policy
   that creates a purchase that would never have happened look identical in this data. Nothing here
   distinguishes them.

3. **One retailer.** One company's list, one catalogue, one pair of creatives. Everything here is a
   statement about this retailer's customers. It is not evidence about another retailer, another
   category, or a list assembled differently.

4. **Cost per email and gross margin are assumptions, not data.** The Hillstrom experiment records
   neither. The app's cost and margin controls are the reader's numbers, not the experiment's, and
   the project adopts no default for either -- there is no cost constant anywhere in the analysis
   code, deliberately, because a fabricated constant would be silently inherited by every profit
   figure downstream. What the project does instead is sweep the cost-to-margin ratio and report where
   the answer changes: the recommended depth does not move at all until that ratio reaches
   `cost_exhibit.first_breakpoint`, and it falls to zero only at
   `cost_exhibit.first_ratio_with_k_star_zero`. Read both numbers out of
   `data/processed/manifest.json` and quote them at three decimals.

5. **The winner's curse on threshold selection.** Name it in those words. The cost-optimal depth k\*
   is chosen by looking at the same customers it is then scored on, so the profit reported at that
   depth is optimistic by construction -- picking the best-looking point on a noisy curve and then
   quoting that point's height is the error, and it is an error no amount of data collection fixes.
   Say what the project does about it: the published headline sits at a capacity anchor fixed and
   committed **before** any policy number existed, not at k\*, and the cost-optimal depth is shown as
   a sensitivity exhibit rather than as a recommendation. Then give the measured size of the related
   optimism rather than asserting it is small: at the published depth a model's own belief about its
   top-k visit effect is `optimism.miscalibration.visit.ratio_at_capacity` times what the
   randomization actually delivered. Quote that ratio at two decimals, the same grain the method
   section uses.

6. **The counterfactual caveat.** Nobody ran this policy. The randomization supports an unbiased
   estimate of what a top-k targeting rule *would have* earned on these `frame.n_customers` customers
   in that fortnight. It does not establish that running the policy for a year produces the same
   effect, that the customers you stop emailing stay unharmed by not being emailed, or that the
   model's ranking is stable enough to re-fit next quarter and get the same list. Say that plainly and
   stop -- the criterion's word is "plainly" and a hedge here is the opposite of what it asks for.

**Then the handoff line.** State that these six are the ones ROADMAP Phase 7 criterion 3 names, not
the whole list, and link `reports/policy.md` section 13, "What would break the claim", as the longer
one. A README that presents six items as exhaustive is making a completeness claim it cannot support,
and §13 carries three more -- the anchor being a convention, the small number of purchase events, and
everything resting on one pre-committed split.

**Four prohibitions, each with its reason:**

- **Do not soften.** No "but", "however", "that said" or "nevertheless" clause that recovers the claim
  inside a bullet. The value of this section is that it reads as though a skeptic wrote it; a bullet
  that argues its way back out reads as though the author did.
- **Do not move D-02's label down here.** The "not detectable at this depth" statement stays in the
  same visual block as the dollar figure on the first screen. `07-CONTEXT.md` marks D-02 LOCKED and
  calls it the phase's load-bearing judgment. This section may refer to the fact; it may not become
  the place a reader first learns it.
- **Do not introduce a number this section does not need.** Four numbers are authorised: the two cost
  breakpoints, the miscalibration ratio, and the frame size. Each is read from `manifest.json`. Any

- **Do not restate `reports/policy.md` §13.** Six short bullets and a link, not eight paragraphs
  rewritten. D-04 is the standing decision and the depth document is already tested.

Commit as its own atomic commit whose body states that criterion 3 names six things by name and that
