<objective>
Put the answer on the first screen, and make it impossible to quote without its uncertainty.

Purpose: this is the phase's core design problem. The app's default view shows a **positive number
the data cannot distinguish from zero** — at the pre-registered `k = 0.20` the shipped ranking's
spend contrast is `+$0.101593` with a 95% interval of `[-$0.029911, +$0.303415]`. A ten-second read
and an honest read are in genuine tension, and the resolution is structural: the ten-second payload
is a **decision sentence**, not a number, and the number that follows it is physically adjacent to
its interval and its verdict inside one bordered container. That adjacency is asserted by index over
flat document order, which is why `st.columns` is forbidden in the main body.

The third verdict state was nearly missed and is reachable: on the shipped ranking's spend contrast
at `k = 0.99` the band is `[-$0.197415, -$0.006580]` around `-$0.088285`. Without it the app prints
a negative number under a line reading "the interval excludes zero", which reads as good news.

No semantic colour signals detectability anywhere. At the anchor the shipped rule's interval covers
zero while the sensitivity-that-was-not-adopted excludes it — a green "significant" badge would
reward a reviewer for switching to the rule this project deliberately did not adopt, which is the
exact winner's-curse selection `reports/policy.md` §11 measures.

Output: the headline block, the two policy curves with their captions, and the tests that pin the
adjacency, the three verdict states and their agreement with the figure's own hatching.
</objective>
