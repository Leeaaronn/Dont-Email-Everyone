  `(arm, outcome, learner)` is 18 rows, matching `ate.parquet`'s tidy precedent;
  `permutation_null.parquet` is long-form at 1,600 rows because the histogram wants one column of
  draws per cell and quantile recomputation is a `groupby`; `scored_holdout.parquet` is wide at one row
  per holdout customer; and `model.json` carries the cross-arm scalars and tie diagnostics whose grain
  matches none of those. This is `pipeline.py`'s own stated rule at lines 32-36: anything whose grain
  is not the table's grain lands in the JSON rather than becoming a fifth and sixth table.
- **`scored_holdout.parquet` is wide at 32,000 rows — one row per holdout customer of the analysis
  table** (04-RESEARCH §Q8). A long table keyed by `(arm, customer)` would be 42,654 rows and would
  duplicate the 10,653 shared control customers, which is the exact structure Phase 5's shared-control
  bootstrap must avoid double-counting. Two per-arm tables would make the shared control invisible in
  the data. The wide form makes the shared control literally one set of rows, so Phase 5 criterion 2's
  "resample the shared control once per replicate" is a `groupby` rather than a join.
- **Score columns are `float32`** (04-RESEARCH §Q8). Measured worst case with random normal fill: 7.7
  MB at `float64` against 4.7 MB at `float32`, and real scores compress far better. `float32` carries
  about seven decimal digits, six orders of magnitude more precision than a ranking or a dollar figure
  needs, and PC-5 budgets a few MB for the Community Cloud load.
- **The response baseline is free** — it *is* `m1`, already fitted (D-13). `response_{arm}_{outcome}`
  is a copy of `m1_{arm}_{outcome}`, and a test asserts the two columns are identical so a future
  refactor cannot silently make the baseline a different model.
- **`n_jobs` stays a literal 1 and `random_state` a literal `20260902`**, so the committed artifacts
  are not thread-count dependent.
