<objective>

calibration check, the D-21 propensity-degeneracy gate, and the D-20 cross-arm incomparability
measurement — with their tests.

Purpose: D-21 is the only mechanism that actually *enforces* this project's "uplift, not propensity"
claim rather than asserting it. A cell could beat the response baseline on Qini while correlating
0.95 with `m0`, and without this gate it would ship as an uplift result. D-22 is the check that
catches a swapped `m0`/`m1` and a mis-scaled score. D-20 hands Phase 5 a *quantified* incomparability
problem instead of an asserted one.

seeds and refit all six cells at each, producing 120 observations, and the resulting per-cell standard

<discretion_resolutions>
- **The magnitude band is `3 x` the measured per-cell seed-to-seed SD, not a relative percentage**
  (04-RESEARCH §Q7). A single relative bar is structurally wrong here: relative error scales inversely
  with effect size and this phase's six effects span three orders of magnitude (0.003111 to 0.769827).
  Measured, `womens/conversion` has the smallest ATE and the largest relative noise (median 21.47%)
  while `mens/visit` has the largest binary ATE and the smallest (median 2.48%). Applying PITFALLS'
  5% bar fails **4 of 6 cells at the median seed and 5 of 6 at the worst**. The multiplier `3` is a
  conventional coverage choice, not a tuned number, and all six cells pass with 1.23x to 1.73x margin.
- **The sign gate is hard and adopted as written in D-22.** Measured 120/120 passes across six cells
  and 20 seeds, with the closest call `womens/spend` at mean_u 0.173 against ATE 0.424 — same sign,
  comfortably nonzero. Sharp enough to catch a swapped `m0`/`m1`, which would flip every sign; loose
  enough never to fire on legitimate seed variation.
- **`calibration_check` compares against the COMMITTED ATE, passed in by the caller** — it never
  recomputes one (04-RESEARCH open question 3). Recomputing would make the check compare a model
  against a number this same phase produced, which is weaker, and 02-06's content canary exists
  precisely so the committed value can be trusted. This also keeps `models.py` pure: the read of
  `ate.parquet` belongs to `pipeline.train()`.
- **`propensity_correlations` reports against BOTH base scores, and the gate is `max(|r|)`.** D-21
  says "against either base-model score", so reporting only one leaves the other unguarded. Measured
  maximum at the primary seed is 0.879 (mens/spend against `m1`); across 20 split seeds the maximum
  reaches 0.9234, which **would fire** the gate. It is not vacuous and the plan budgets for it firing
  on a spend cell.
- **No rescaling is implemented (D-20).** Mean-matching each arm to its own ATE was considered and
  rejected: an affine rescale does not fix rank-level incomparability, and it would make ROADMAP
  criterion 4's calibration check trivially true by construction, destroying its value as a check.
</discretion_resolutions>

<blocker_resolution>
STATE.md carries the open blocker: *"Whether a genuine negative-uplift segment survives holdout
validation on the Mens arm is unknown. Settle empirically; do not assume either answer."*

04-RESEARCH §Q7 measured it. **On the mens arm, no** — the minimum predicted visit uplift on the
shared control holdout is **+0.046469**, strictly positive, so the primary learner predicts no
negative-uplift customers for the mens email. **On the womens arm, yes** — the minimum is
**-0.070802** and a nontrivial subgroup has predicted negative uplift. The blocker was phrased as a
mens-arm question and the answer is that the phenomenon appears on the *other* arm. `cross_arm_metrics`
must therefore report the per-arm **minimum** predicted uplift and the negative fraction, not only the
mean and SD, so the finding is carried in the data rather than only in prose. Plan 04-09 writes it up
and notes the arm swap explicitly.
</blocker_resolution>
