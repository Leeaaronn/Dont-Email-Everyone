<critical_no_fabrication>
**The write-up must NOT claim a stray significant per-covariate p-value.** ROADMAP Phase 2 success criterion #2 and CONTEXT.md D-04 both describe one; it does not exist in this data. All 21 p-values are at or above 0.19377.

The roadmap sentence compressed a correct statement about an *expectation* ("with 21 tests you expect about one significant result by chance") into a description of an *outcome*. Under the null with 21 tests, P(zero significant) is about 34% — getting zero is unremarkable.

Write it as a **pre-registered decision rule stated before the result**, in this shape:

> Acceptance criterion, stated before the analysis: randomization is accepted if (a) every |SMD| is below 0.1 across all three pairwise comparisons, and (b) the omnibus multinomial-logit likelihood-ratio test does not reject at alpha = 0.05. Per-covariate p-values are reported for completeness but are not part of the criterion: with 21 tests, roughly one p-value below 0.05 is expected under perfect randomization, so a single significant covariate would not have overturned the conclusion. As it happens none occurred — the smallest of the 21 p-values is 0.194.

That satisfies the roadmap's intent (demonstrating the author knows why per-covariate p-values are the wrong instrument here) while remaining true. This is a portfolio piece whose entire value proposition is defensibility; reporting a finding that does not exist is the failure mode that actually costs something.

Two further prose constraints:
- Write "HC2 reduces exactly to the Welch standard error in the two-group case, and HC3 is the small-sample-conservative member of the same White family, agreeing with Welch to five decimal places at these sample sizes." Do **not** write "HC3 is Welch."
- Do **not** write that the t-test is wrong because spend is zero-inflated. At full arm size the bootstrap and the analytic interval agree to roughly one cent; the honest claim is that the Welch interval is fine here and degrades only at much smaller cell sizes, which is what the coverage table quantifies.
</critical_no_fabrication>
