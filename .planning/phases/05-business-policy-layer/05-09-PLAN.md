<objective>
Write the phase's evidence document, and have a human check that it says what is true.

Purpose: `reports/model.md` set the standard — every number sourced at three tiers with the
tier named at the point of use, gates stated above results, and anti-overclaim rules enforced
by tests that assert the qualifier sits in the SAME PASSAGE as the number it qualifies. This
document has a harder job: its central contrast is negative, its headline is a comparator the
project's own title does not name, and one of its most interesting numbers comes from a policy
it explicitly does not ship.

The intellectual move the document exists to make explicit — 05-CONTEXT.md's Specific Ideas
section says a reader must not be left to infer it — is the **separation of the ranking device
from the value estimator**. A policy's value does not have to be estimated with the quantity
used to rank; IPW gives an unbiased value for whatever ranking it is handed. That is why "your
spend model failed its null" is an objection to something the headline does not use.

Output: `reports/policy.md` complete, its test coverage, and an approved accuracy checkpoint.
</objective>

Sections required, in an order that puts every rule above every result:

1. **The question, and what this document answers.** The project's title asks how much more
   revenue a targeted campaign generates than emailing everyone. State early and plainly that
   this document answers that question and that the answer is not the one the title implies —
   with the reason being arithmetic, not model failure.

2. **The estimator, and the separation it rests on.** The ranking device and the value
   estimator are deliberately different things (D-01, D-02). Known-propensity IPW on actual
   holdout outcomes, never a sum of predicted uplift. Write out the three quantities and both
   difference identities.

3. **The weight, derived rather than transcribed (D-16).** Design propensity 1/3; the womens
   uplift is NaN on all 10,654 mens holdout rows, so the policy is evaluated on the two-arm
   frame; conditioning on a randomly assigned variable induces no selection bias;
   `P(womens | {w,c}) = 1/2`, so the weight is 2. State the measured cost of transcribing 3:
   V(email everyone) reported as $1.7227/customer against an actual arm mean of $1.1462, an
   implied ATE that does not match `ate.parquet`. Note that ROADMAP criterion 1 was amended in
   place to say the weight is derived from the frame.

4. **Units, named once and then used precisely.** Three units live in this phase: `Q(k)` per
   treated customer over the whole population; the policy contrasts per POPULATION customer of
   the 21,347-row frame; the per-targeted figure per EMAIL SENT. State that the per-targeted
   figure divides by `int(n*k)` — the realized email count — and not by the exact `k`, and give
   both numbers at the anchor so the choice reads as resolved rather than unnoticed.

5. **The headline (D-08a).** Targeted top-k against a RANDOM send of the same size, with its
   interval, at the pre-registered anchor, for visit and spend. **The zero-cost caveat sits in

   everyone; this result is about spending a fixed budget well. Beside it, the crossover from
   the cost exhibit. A test asserts the adjacency, not merely the presence.

6. **Both criterion-1 contrasts, reported honestly.** vs email-nobody and vs email-everyone,
   with intervals, and the identity `delta_all(k) = -(incremental outcome of the bottom 1-k)`
   as the explanation. State that the vs-everyone contrast's CI covers zero at every k on this
   data and is significantly negative at small k, that this follows from the womens arm having
   a positive ATE on every outcome, and that no anchor choice could have changed it. This
   paragraph converts a disappointing number into a result; write it that way.

7. **The full grid (D-07).** Point at `policy_curve.parquet` and the two committed figures, and
   state that any capacity can be read off them.

8. **The head of the curve is unstable, and here is how unstable.** Re-measure and report: the
   number of non-zero spend rows in the evaluation frame, the conversion count per k-slice
   below 0.15, and the largest single purchase. State that any anchor below roughly 0.125 is a
   statement about a dozen shopping trips.

9. **Ratios are point estimates.** Report the efficiency ratio (top-k per email against a
   blanket send per email) and the capture fraction as POINT ESTIMATES derived from two
   intervals, and say in the same passage why they are not published as intervals: the
   denominator is itself an estimate, and the measured CIs cover zero. Do not print those two
   CIs as if they were results.

10. **The cost exhibit (D-09).** k\* against c/m, the measured first breakpoint, and the
    selection caveat: k\* is chosen by maximizing over the evaluation rows and therefore
    carries exactly the optimism the exogenous anchor avoids, so it is an exhibit and never
    the headline, and it is never quoted without its (cost, margin). Add the finding the
    insensitivity actually is: optimal depth barely moves across plausible email costs because
    email is nearly free relative to the incremental purchase it produces.

11. **The winner's curse, measured (D-05).** The naive-vs-honest table for the womens-only
    policy, the Jensen gap on the shared control rows, the argmax policy's honest value at
    weight 3 on the 32,001-row frame, and the decomposition
    `winners_curse = gap_argmax - gap_womens`. State that aggregate calibration and top-k
    honesty are different properties, and that the cell in question passes the first and fails
    the second. Every number here carries its `unproven_` label. State D-04 explicitly: this
    policy is measured and is NOT shipped, because it rests on mens rankings that failed their
    own nulls — and the measured argmax-picks-mens share is that justification as data.

12. **The sensitivity that was not adopted (Pitfall 5).** `uplift_womens_conversion` beats the
    locked D-01 ranking on the revenue objective at small k. Publish the curve, and then say
    why it was not adopted: D-01 was locked on Phase 4 evidence before this curve existed, and
    switching now would be selection on the evaluation rows — the exact winner's curse section
    11 measures. That paragraph is worth more than the swap would be.

13. **What would break the claim.** A skeptic's list: 2008 vintage, a single two-week window,
    one retailer, cost and margin as assumptions rather than data, the anchor as a convention,
    and the counterfactual caveat. Phase 7's README will draw on this; write it here where the

14. **Inputs and artifacts.** Every committed file with a one-line description, plus the
