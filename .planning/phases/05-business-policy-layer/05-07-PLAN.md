<objective>
Discharge D-05: measure the winner's curse rather than assert it.

Purpose: Phase 4's D-19 delivered a quantified incomparability and explicitly deferred the
policy decision to this phase. 05-CONTEXT.md rules out restating the caution qualitatively —
that would discharge nothing. Most portfolio projects assert that an argmax over correlated
estimates is optimistic; measuring the size of that bias is the differentiator, and
05-CONTEXT.md's Specific Ideas section calls it a headline-worthy finding in its own right.

Two things must be separated, and this plan's whole design is that separation:

    gap_womens    = naive_womens - honest_womens      miscalibration only
    gap_argmax    = naive_argmax - honest_argmax      miscalibration + winner's curse
    winners_curse = gap_argmax - gap_womens           the isolated finding

Measured during research on the womens-only side: at the anchor, the
`unproven_uplift_womens_spend` model believes it is delivering +$1.4921 per targeted
customer against a measured +$0.7446 — a 2.00x overstatement — while being essentially
perfectly calibrated in aggregate (ratio 1.00 at k = 1, consistent with that cell's
`calibration_pass = True`). Aggregate calibration and top-k honesty are different properties
and this exhibit separates them.

D-04 is unchanged by any of this: the shipped policy still targets the womens arm only,
because the argmax rests on mens rankings that failed their own nulls. The measured
"argmax picks mens 78.7-86.0% of the time" is D-04's justification restated as data.

Output: three new estimators in `evaluation.py`, an `optimism` block and an
`estimator_robustness` block in `manifest.json`.
</objective>

  <action>
Add three public functions to `evaluation.py`, in the ranking-and-randomization tier where

**`argmax_policy_value`** — the honest side. Its docstring must carry, in full, the reason it
could not be computed before plan 05-03: IPW's numerator counts rows whose realized arm
matches the prescribed action, and for an argmax policy those are the TREATED rows, which are
exactly the rows where the other arm's score used to be NaN. Also record why a holdout split
does not repair it — the winner's curse here comes from estimation noise in a single fitted
`û` that is common to every holdout row, so two halves of the holdout share that noise and a
within-holdout split measures nothing. And record why a surrogate classifier imputing the
argmax label on control rows was rejected: it adds a model to a phase whose entire argument is
that no model enters the value estimate.

**The weight on this frame is 3, and `N` is 32,001.** This is the ONE place criterion 1's
literal "1/3" applies unmodified: on the full holdout every action in {mens, womens, no-email}
is realized with known probability 1/3. State the contrast with `POLICY_WEIGHT = 2.0` in the
docstring in one sentence, so a reader who sees both weights in one module understands that
each is a property of the frame its estimator runs on. Do not add a second module constant
that could drift from the first; take the weight as a parameter with the derivation in the
docstring.

**`naive_policy_value`** — the model's own belief, `mean(max(û_mens, û_womens))`. Docstring:
this is the quantity D-02 forbids as a headline; it exists here only so the gap against the
honest estimate can be measured. Add the Jensen decomposition in words:
`mean(max) >= max(mean)` always, so part of the gap is arithmetic (the Jensen gap) and part is
the winner's curse; the exhibit reports the Jensen gap separately for exactly that reason.

**`policy_value_variants`** — HT, Hajek and AIPW at one k. Docstring must record what each
buys, measured: Hajek reproduces the arm means exactly where HT does not, at the cost of being
a ratio estimator; AIPW using the committed `m0`/`m1` columns narrows the interval by about
0.4%, because the base models explain essentially none of spend's variance. Conclusion in the
docstring: HT is criterion 1's literal reading, it reduces to a two-term subtraction a reader
can check, and it stays the headline estimator; the other two are a one-line robustness note

`test_evaluation_module_writes_nothing`'s call list and its running comment.
