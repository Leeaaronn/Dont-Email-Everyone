  <behavior>
    - `profit_curve(delta_none, grid, cost_per_email=c, gross_margin=m)` returns `m * delta_none - c * grid`, elementwise.
    - Omitting `cost_per_email` or `gross_margin` raises `TypeError` — they are required keyword-only arguments with no defaults.
    - On a synthetic concave curve with a known analytic maximum, `optimal_k` returns that maximum's grid point.
    - `optimal_k` on a tie returns the SMALLEST k among the tied maxima, and the rule is stated in the docstring.
    - Over a synthetic curve and a ratio sweep, `optimal_k` is monotonically non-increasing in `cost/margin` and takes at least 4 distinct values.
    - `optimal_k` depends on `cost` and `margin` only through their ratio: `optimal_k(c=0.2, m=1.0) == optimal_k(c=0.1, m=0.5)`.
    - Raises `ValueError` on `gross_margin <= 0`, on `cost_per_email < 0`, on non-finite inputs, and on `delta_none`/`grid` of mismatched length.
  </behavior>
  <action>
Add three public functions to `economics.py`. No RNG, no ranking, no import of
`evaluation` — the module boundary rule is that `economics.py` owns money and nothing else,
which is what keeps its tests closed-form.

    def profit_curve(delta_none, grid, *, cost_per_email: float, gross_margin: float):
        """Profit per population customer: margin * incremental outcome - cost * k."""

    def optimal_k(delta_none, grid, *, cost_per_email: float, gross_margin: float):
        """Return (k_star, profit_at_k_star) maximizing profit_curve."""

    def cost_margin_sweep(delta_none, grid, ratios):
        """Return (ratios, k_star, profit_at_k_star) over a c/m ratio grid."""

Design notes that must land in the code, not only in this plan:

  - **No defaults.** `cost_per_email` and `gross_margin` are keyword-only and have no
    default value anywhere in the module. Write the D-10 reasoning in the docstring: named
    industry values ($0.10/email, 40% retail margin) were considered and rejected as
    defaults because both would be invented; they remain reasonable ILLUSTRATIVE values
    inside the sweep, and the sweep's job is to show k\* moving rather than to adopt one.
  - **The tie rule, stated.** `np.argmax` returns the FIRST maximum, so ties resolve to the
    smallest k — the cheapest campaign among equally profitable ones. Say that is the
    intended rule and that it is intended, not incidental.
  - **Only the ratio matters.** `Pi(k) = m * delta - c * k` is `m * (delta - (c/m) * k)`, so
    k\* depends on `c` and `m` only through `c/m`. That is why `cost_margin_sweep` takes
    ratios rather than a two-dimensional grid, and why the exhibit's axis is labelled `c/m`.
    Put this derivation in the docstring; it is the reason the exhibit is one-dimensional.
  - **The selection caveat.** k\* is chosen by maximizing over the evaluation grid and is
    therefore an optimistic estimate of the best depth — the exact optimism the exogenous
    `HEADLINE_CAPACITY` anchor avoids. Record in the `optimal_k` docstring that D-09 makes
    this an exhibit and never a headline, and that k\* must never be quoted without its
    `(cost, margin)` attached.
  - **The honest range.** Record the measured fact that k\* is flat until roughly
    c/m = 0.068, so at a realistic $0.001/email and 40% margin (c/m = 0.0025) nothing moves.
    Note in the docstring that this insensitivity is itself the business finding — email is
    nearly free relative to the incremental purchase it produces — and that the sweep must
    therefore be labelled in `c/m` rather than dressed up with one adopted cost.

Guards raise named `ValueError`s and never `assert`. Follow the `_guard_*` shape already in
`evaluation.py`, with each message naming the offending value and what would have gone wrong.
  </action>
